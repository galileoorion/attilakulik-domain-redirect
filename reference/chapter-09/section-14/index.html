  • Said, Edward W. Beginnings: Intention and Method. New York: Basic Books, 1975.
  • Smith, Barbara Herrnstein. Poetic Closure: A Study of How Poems End. Chicago: University of Chicago Press, 1968.

  • Suvin, Darko. Metamorphoses of Science Fiction: On the Poetics and History of a Literary Genre. New Haven: Yale University Press, 1979.
  • Watt, Ian. The Rise of the Novel: Studies in Defoe, Richardson and Fielding. London: Chatto & Windus, 1957.
  • Wood, James. How Fiction Works. London: Jonathan Cape, 2008.

14.5 Second-edition sources

Examples added in the second edition (Core chapter) and the sources used to verify them.

  • Herbert, Frank. Dune. 1965. (The gom jabbar held at Paul’s neck by Mohiam, the “old crone” of the opening, in the first chapter; Alia kills Baron Harkonnen with it at the climax: Wikipedia, “Gaius Helen Mohiam”; Inverse, on the Baron’s death in the novel.)
  • Honeyman, Gail. Eleanor Oliphant Is Completely Fine. London: HarperCollins, 2017. (First-person narrator, Glasgow, a finance clerk of twenty-nine; Costa Debut Novel Award 2017; opening sentence quoted on the Reading Between the Pages blog: Wikipedia, “Eleanor Oliphant Is Completely Fine”.)
  • Ng, Celeste. Everything I Never Told You. New York: Penguin Press, 2014. (Opening sentences; 3 May 1977, Lydia’s bed not slept in, her body recovered from the lake: Course Hero, chapter 1 summary.)
  • Saunders, George. Lincoln in the Bardo. 2017. (Opening sentence spoken by Hans Vollman; passages attributed to named speakers; chapters assembled from quotations of real and invented sources: LitCharts, chapter 1; The Post Calvin.)
  • Weir, Andy. Project Hail Mary. 2021. (Opening lines, “What’s two plus two?”; the narrator wakes without memory beside two long-dead crewmates and recovers his name only in chapter 3: BookBrowse excerpt; The Bibliofile recap.)
  • Inglourious Basterds. Dir. Quentin Tarantino, 2009. (Chapter One, “Once upon a time… in Nazi-occupied France”, 1941; LaPadite chopping wood; Landa asks for milk, switches to English; the Dreyfus family under the floor; Shosanna escapes: Inglourious Basterds wiki, “Chapter One”.)
  • The Sopranos, season 1, episode 1, “The Sopranos” (the pilot). HBO, 10 January 1999; written and directed by David Chase. (Opens in Dr Melfi’s waiting room with Tony and a statue; the episode framed by the sessions, with his account shown in flashback; the ducks: Wikipedia; TV Tropes recap.)
