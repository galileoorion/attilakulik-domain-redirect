Chapter 9 · The Opening Pages

10. Openings Across Forms

Continue in Core

The contract model was developed for the novel. It transfers to adjacent forms with adjustments, because students frequently import novel-opening habits into forms that punish them.

10.1 The short story

The story has proportionally less room and more urgency, and it resolves the shortfall by an important structural difference: the short story may legitimately open at its central situation rather than approaching it.

Difference Consequence
No room for a hybrid approach The attack is typically at or immediately before the crisis
The world contract may be nearly waived A story can occupy a single room and never establish a wider world
The formal contract is discharged instantly Length itself is a formal declaration
Titles carry more weight In stories, the title is often a working part of the opening rather than paratext

The critical difference is that a story’s opening is also its middle. Where a novel’s opening buys the reader’s continuation, a story’s opening frequently constitutes a substantial fraction of the whole, and therefore must do contract work and plot work in the same sentences with no possibility of deferral.

10.2 The novella

The novella’s characteristic opening problem is that it is written by novelists. It has a novel’s tonal amplitude and a story’s word count, and openings that spend three pages on approach have spent a twentieth of the book. The Death of Ivan Ilyich is the model case: Tolstoy opens with the announcement of the death and the colleagues’ reaction to it — the ending first, the whole moral apparatus of the book declared in its first pages, and no approach at all.

10.3 Multi-viewpoint and braided structures

Covered at §4.6, and restated here as a rule because it is the most common structural failure in ambitious drafts:

Declare the pattern before the reader has invested in a single thread. In practice: the first viewpoint change should occur at the first chapter break, not at page ninety.

The Sound and the Fury and As I Lay Dying both declare polyphony immediately — Faulkner’s section headings are the declaration. Bleak House declares by contrast at Chapter III. Wide Sargasso Sea declares by voice change at its first major break. None waits.

10.4 Series instalments

Series openings face a genuine dilemma: they must serve a returning reader who wants continuity and a new reader who has arrived at volume three. The common failure is to serve neither — a recap paragraph that bores the first and does not orient the second.

Three principles:

  1. Assume less prior knowledge than feels reasonable. Readers who finished the previous volume eighteen months ago retain the emotional shape and almost none of the plot mechanics.
  2. Re-establish the voice contract completely. It costs almost nothing and is what returning readers are actually returning for.
  3. Deliver recap as present-tense consequence, not as summary. Not after the events of last winter, but a character behaving in a way the previous volume explains and this one demonstrates.

10.5 A caution on borrowing from screen

Contemporary craft advice imports heavily from film — “cold open”, “establishing shot”, “cut in late”. Two of those import badly.

  • The establishing shot is free in film and expensive in prose. A camera shows a city in one second and at no narrative cost; a paragraph describing a city costs the reader a paragraph and, per §3.2, promises that the city matters.
  • Film has involuntary orientation. The viewer sees faces, ages, weather, period and class simultaneously, without the director spending anything. Prose orients only what it names, in sequence. This is the structural reason novels need the hybrid of §6.4 more than films need it.

What does transfer is the principle of late entry into a scene — beginning after the conversation has started — which works in both media for the same reason.
