The Ending

The Permanent Stopping Place: Theory, Analysis and Practice

The Craft Behind the Story (Second Edition), Reference — Chapter 16. Part III: The Architecture of the Whole.

A companion to the other chapters of this book and, in particular, the formal inverse of Chapter 9 (The Opening Pages), whose argument this chapter completes from the other end of the book.

All illustrative passages are quoted briefly for the purposes of criticism and study; full citations appear in §14. Where a work is read in translation, the translator is named, and the chapter observes the book’s distinction: structural claims survive translation, texture claims do not.

In the first edition this was the final chapter, and its coda, §16, was written to close the book as well as the chapter; both are kept as written. In this edition Chapter 18 (The Structural Edit) closes the book, and this chapter closes Part III.

Chapter Abstract

An ending is the only unit of a novel with no subsequent scene to correct it. Every other unit in this textbook operates inside a story still running, and can therefore be rescued by what follows: a slack chapter is redeemed by the one after it, a confusing transition is clarified two pages later, a scene whose point is invisible acquires one retrospectively. The ending has no such recourse. It is also the unit through which the reader re-reads everything that came before, so that its failures are not local but retroactive — they reach backwards and reassign meaning to four hundred pages that cannot answer back.

This chapter formalises that asymmetry into a teachable model, and it does so in awareness of having been written as the last chapter of the first edition; in this edition it closes Part III, and two chapters follow it, on genre and series and on the structural edit. Every instrument the earlier chapters supplied — the value shift, the causal chain, proposition and payoff, the attention tax, closing the distance, the contracts — comes due here, in the way an ending’s plants come due. The chapter is therefore both an account of endings and a demonstration of one.

It proceeds in four movements:

  1. Theory — nine strands establishing why the last pages carry disproportionate weight: Kermode on endings as fictions of concord; Herrnstein Smith on the difference between closure and mere termination; Aristotle on necessity, probability and the unravelling; Brooks on the anticipation of retrospection; Torgovnick on congruence and the forms of the close; the cognitive literature on recency, privileged position and the peak–end rule; the history of the ending from the Victorian settlement to the contemporary open close; DuPlessis on whose endings were available to whom; and D. A. Miller’s argument that closure is always in some measure a betrayal of what made the book worth reading.
  2. Taxonomy — the five domains of an ending’s purpose (plot resolution, character-arc completion, thematic closure, emotional catharsis, the final image), each anatomised through its textual carriers, its under- and over-delivery, and published illustrations — expanded here with a proposed sixth, formal closure: the completion of the book’s own structural pattern rather than its story, which is what makes Nineteen Eighty-Four’s Appendix and The Handmaid’s Tale’s Historical Notes so disruptive.
  3. Method — the principle of multiplicity applied at the highest available stakes; the earned/unearned distinction, analysed as a claim about preparation and retrospective inevitability rather than about predictability; a taxonomy of sixteen closural strategies; the proportion problem of climax and aftermath; and a ten-test diagnostic protocol for revision.
  4. Application — twelve failure patterns, each with a published counterexample that commits the error and succeeds; nine close readings, centred on the two endings of Great Expectations; the ending across forms; a capstone revision case that audits the household narrative’s plants across the entire textbook, rerun across the second edition’s eighteen chapters in section 9.10.8; and a full apparatus of exercises, glossary, sources and further reading.

The chapter’s governing claim is that an ending is not a stopping place but a retroactive lens — the unit through which the whole book is finally read — and that the practical consequence is a discipline of scarcity: an ending may spend only what the book has already earned the right to spend.

The chapter’s governing caution is that this discipline assumes closure is desirable. A serious critical tradition holds that it is not — that the ending is where a novel domesticates the very instability that made it interesting. That objection is put at full strength in §3.9 and answered, partially and without evasion, in §6.1 and §16.

A note on the examples. The illustrative endings are drawn mostly from widely read novels. A concordance mapping each concept to the novel that best displays it appears at §10.7. The second edition adds post-2000 and screen cases, listed in §14.5, and treats several of them in the Core chapter.

A note on spoilers. This chapter discusses the endings of some forty novels in detail, and cannot do otherwise. That is a real cost, and students should be warned before it is imposed on them. It is also a lesser cost than it appears: the argument of §6.2 is that a strong ending is not damaged by being known in advance, because inevitability and surprise are different properties. Whether the reader’s experience of Great Expectations survives knowing how it ends — twice — is one of the questions the chapter puts to the class.

Learning Outcomes

By the end of this chapter, a student should be able to:

  • Distinguish the ending from the units nearest to it — the climax, the denouement, the epilogue, the coda, the last line — and explain why each distinction changes a revision decision.
  • Separate resolution (the plot’s questions answered, or legibly declined) from closure (the felt sense of completion), and analyse the four possible combinations of the two.
  • Explain, with reference to at least four theoretical sources, why the final pages of a narrative exert influence disproportionate to their length, and state accurately what the cognitive evidence does and does not support.
  • Identify which of the six domains an ending serves, which it merely gestures towards, and which it deliberately declines.
  • Analyse the earned/unearned distinction as a claim about preparation and retrospective inevitability, and demonstrate why an ending may be entirely unforeseen and still earned.
  • Apply a taxonomy of sixteen closural strategies to a draft, and use substitution testing to discover which strategy the material actually wants.
  • Conduct a whole-book plant audit: trace every element an ending relies on back to its planting, and identify anything load-bearing that arrives for the first time at the close.
  • Diagnose the recurring failure patterns by mechanism rather than by surface feature, and argue for the conditions under which each “failure” is the correct choice.
  • Evaluate the limits of the model itself — including the possibility, argued by D. A. Miller and by the whole tradition of the open ending, that the demand for an earned close is a demand the best novels are right to refuse.

How to Use This Chapter

Reader Route Purpose
Student in a seminar §§1–6 in order, then §9.1 (the Great Expectations case), then §12 Conceptual grounding, the discipline’s best single case study, then guided practice
Writer in revision §7 (diagnostics) → §8 (failures) → §6.4 (strategies) → §11 (checklist) Fast triage of a live ending, ending in a decision about strategy and proportion
Teacher or workshop leader §§3.7–3.9, 6.4, 8, 9 for material; §12.1 for assignments; §12.3 rubric A teaching sequence that includes the historical and political correctives, not only the craft rule

A caution before beginning, in the same terms as Chapter 9 (The Opening Pages) and Chapter 10 (The Prologue). This chapter contains taxonomies, tests and checklists. None of them is a formula. They are instruments for noticing, and their proper output is a question. A protocol that returns the answer “this ending fails test six” has done nothing until the writer asks whether the book wants it to.

That caution carries more weight here than anywhere else in the textbook, for a reason that belongs at the outset. The instruments in this chapter are calibrated to endings that conclude. A substantial and distinguished body of fiction does not want to conclude, and the model will register its refusals as failures. When it does, the model is reporting on itself.
