Chapter 16 · The Ending

12. Studio Work

Continue in Core

12.1 Practical exercises

Exercise 1 — The plant audit, in full. Take a completed draft. List every element the ending uses as load-bearing (aim for twelve to twenty). For each, record the chapter and page of first appearance and of every intermediate appearance. Produce the table in the form of §9.10.4. Write 300 words on what the distribution shows — in particular, on any element that appears once and then not again for a third of the book.

Exercise 2 — Strategy substitution. Take one draft ending and write three versions using three strategies from §6.4, chosen from different clusters — for example 2 (parallelism), 7 (epilogue in another voice) and 12 (arrested). Write a page of each. Do not judge until all three exist. Then write 400 words on what each version makes the book about.

Exercise 3 — The deletion ladder, from the other end. End the book one paragraph earlier; read. One scene earlier; read. One chapter earlier; read. Record where the ending actually is, and what the deleted material was doing for you rather than for the reader. This is Chapter 9 §12.1’s exercise 3 run in reverse, and it has a comparable hit rate.

Exercise 4 — The last-line laboratory. Write ten different final sentences for the same ending: one aphoristic, one an image, one dialogue, one a single word, one interrogative, one very long, one very short, one in the future tense, one in the present, one that names an object. Read them aloud. Record which closes hardest, and which is right — they will not always be the same, and the difference is the exercise.

Exercise 5 — The domain annotation. Run the §5.5 closural audit on the last three pages of a draft. Produce the letter distribution and the inert-sentence count. Write 200 words interpreting it against the table in §5.5.

Exercise 6 — The two endings. Following §9.1, write two endings for the same story: one congruent with what the material implies, one congruent with what the reader wants. Do not label them. Give both to three readers and ask which is better and which is right. Record any case where a reader separates the two answers, and write 300 words on it.

Exercise 7 — The counterfactual ending. Write a deliberately unearned ending for your own draft — one that resolves by a new element, in the manner of §9.10.5 — and then diagnose it against §7.1. The exercise is not satirical: writing the failure deliberately is the most reliable way to see the mechanism, and students routinely discover that their real ending shares one of its defects.

Exercise 8 — Climax and aftermath, measured. Take three published novels from different periods. For each, locate the climax and measure the aftermath as a percentage of the whole. Compare against §6.6’s table. Then do the same for your own draft and write 200 words on whether your proportion is a choice or an inheritance.

Exercise 9 — The unavailable ending. Following §3.8, take a nineteenth-century novel with a female protagonist and write the ending the form did not permit — the one in which she neither marries nor dies. Then write 400 words on what had to change earlier in the book to make your ending possible. The point of the exercise is in the second part: closural forms constrain plots, not only conclusions.

12.2 Seminar questions

  1. The two endings of Great Expectations. Read both (Forster’s Life and every scholarly edition print the cancelled version). Argue for one. You must address: the plant audit, which favours the revision; the congruence criterion, which is usually taken to favour the original; and the significance — if any — of Bulwer-Lytton’s intervention. A strong answer will explain why the disagreement has survived a century and a half rather than declaring it resolved.

  2. Five domains or six? Argue for or against admitting formal closure (§4.6) as a domain distinct from thematic closure. Use the Appendix to Nineteen Eighty-Four as the test case, and address the objection at §4.6 that formal closure names a means rather than an end.

  3. Does Part Eight belong to Anna Karenina? Is Levin’s conclusion a second arc completing (§9.4) or an authorial conclusion appended (§8.6)? Tolstoy’s serial publisher declined to print it; readers have complained ever since. Make the case that they were right.

  4. Is the unresolved close a liberation or a successor convention? §3.7 argues that the ambiguous ending is now the house style of a genre and §3.8 that closural forms encode assumptions about what a life is for. Has contemporary literary fiction escaped the marriage-or-death binary, or exchanged it for a default of its own that will look equally unexamined in fifty years?

  5. Is Miller right? §3.9 puts the case that closure is a betrayal of the narratable and §6.1 attempts an answer. Test both against a single novel of your choosing. Is the ending of that novel a containment, an exchange, or something the distinction does not capture?

  6. The Phelps farm. Does the last quarter of Adventures of Huckleberry Finn resolve the wrong question (§8.7), or does it answer the right one in a way readers have wanted to escape? Address Hemingway’s verdict and at least one defence of the ending.

12.3 Assessment rubric

For a submitted ending (1,500–2,500 words, with a one-page summary of the preceding novel) and a 1,000-word critical commentary.

Band Craft (60%) Commentary (40%)
Distinction Every load-bearing element traceable to an earlier planting; at least one carrier serving three or more domains; proportion between climax and aftermath evidently chosen; a last line that does work the preceding paragraph does not; any ambiguity specific and defensible Names its closural strategy and defends it against two alternatives actually attempted; runs the plant audit on its own work and reports an unredeemed plant; engages at least three theoretical sources critically, including one that tells against the submission
Merit Most elements planted; domains served but with one carrier per domain; proportion defensible; final image present and functional Accurate self-analysis; strategy identified; risks acknowledged if not resolved; theory applied rather than cited
Pass An ending exists that closes the book’s central question; some domains served; nothing fatally unearned Describes the ending accurately; some engagement with the taxonomy; the plant audit attempted
Fail The ending rests on an unplanted element; the book stops rather than ends; the final note contradicts the material without evident purpose Description without analysis; no plant audit; failure patterns present and unrecognised
