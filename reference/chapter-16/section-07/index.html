Chapter 16 · The Ending

7. Diagnostic Method

Continue in Core

7.0 How to use the protocol

The ten tests below are for revision, not composition. That instruction is standard across this textbook and it is more emphatic here, for a specific reason: the ending is the unit whose obligations cannot be known until the rest of the book exists. A writer who applies the plant audit while drafting will write a book made of plants.

Five procedural notes, one more than the other chapters, because this protocol has a peculiarity the others do not.

  • Run them in order. Tests 1–4 are about the ending’s relation to the book, and their failures invalidate the rest: there is no point tuning a last line in an ending that rests on an unplanted element.
  • Record the answer, not the verdict. The output of a test is a sentence describing what the ending does, not a pass or fail.
  • A failed test is a question. Every test below is failed by at least one novel discussed in this chapter, most of them by several.
  • Several of these tests cannot be run on the ending alone. This is the peculiarity. Tests 1, 2, 6 and 9 require the whole book — which means the protocol takes days rather than hours, and which is why writers skip them and why skipping them is, in this book’s judgement, a common reason that unearned endings survive into print.
  • Revise the ending last, whatever order it was written in. Many novelists write the ending first, and there is nothing wrong with that. But an ending written first is a hypothesis about the book, and the book that actually got written may have falsified it. A damaging revision error, and a frequent one, is the writer who protects an early-written ending and bends the last hundred pages towards it. Chapter 9 §7.2 gives the equivalent warning about openings and it applies with more force here: the ending is twenty pages; the book is four hundred.

7.1 The ten tests

Test 1 — The retroactive test

Reread the book’s earlier scenes with the ending in mind. Do they now read as intentional?

Procedure. Take five scenes from the first two-thirds of the book, chosen at random rather than for convenience. Read each and ask what the ending has made of it. Write one sentence per scene: in the light of the ending, this scene was ___.

Interpretation. Three outcomes.

  • Every scene acquires a function: the ending is doing its retroactive work.
  • Some scenes acquire a function and some are unchanged: normal and healthy. Not every scene need be retroactively transformed; most books have a great deal of good material that simply is what it was.
  • Scenes are stranded — they now read as irrelevant, or worse, as belonging to a different book. This is the diagnostic result, and it points in two directions. Either the ending is wrong, or the scene is. The choice between those is the largest editorial decision in a revision, and it should be made explicitly rather than by default.

The test’s characteristic false positive is worth flagging: a scene that seems stranded may be doing tonal or textural work that has no plot correlate. The refinement is to ask whether the reader would miss it, not whether it feeds the ending.

Test 2 — The plant audit

Trace every element the ending relies on back to its planting.

Procedure. This is the most mechanical test in the chapter and the most reliable. It is also the test that closes this entire textbook, because every earlier chapter’s material comes due at it.

  1. List every element the ending uses as load-bearing — every object, fact, capability, relationship, phrase or piece of knowledge without which the ending would not work. Aim for eight to twenty items. If the list is under five, the ending may be under-built; if it is over thirty, the ending is probably doing too much.
  2. For each, record the page and chapter of first appearance, and the pages of any intermediate appearances.
  3. Mark any element whose first appearance is in the ending itself.
  4. Mark any element whose first appearance is early and which then disappears for more than a third of the book.

Interpretation.

Finding Diagnosis Move
An element first appears in the ending Unearned in the strict sense (§6.1, Claim 1) Plant it earlier, unremarked. This is usually one sentence
An element appears once, very early, and not again Planted but not maintained; the reader’s frame has gone cold Add one intermediate appearance (Chapter 10’s instalment schedule)
Every element traces cleanly and the ending still feels flat Claim 1 satisfied, Claim 2 not (§6.1) The problem is not here. Go to test 9
The list is dominated by information rather than by objects, relationships and decisions The ending is a disclosure rather than an event Consider whether the book’s climax is a revelation that ought to be an action

The one-sentence version of this test, which is the form to remember: nothing new and load-bearing arrives on the last pages. Note the qualifier, per §6.3: new material is fine, new load is not.

Test 3 — The earned test

Does the ending depend on a new element — new information, a sudden capability, an outside intervention?

Procedure. Test 2 finds unplanted elements; this test asks whether the resolution mechanism itself is imported. Complete the sentence: “The situation is resolved because ___.” Then ask whether the thing in the blank was produced by the book’s own causal system or arrived from outside it (§6.3).

Interpretation. Three characteristic failures, in ascending order of severity:

  • New information. A letter, a document, a confession that supplies a fact the reader could not have had. Sometimes legitimate — detective fiction is built on it — but only where the possibility of the fact was established.
  • New capability. A character does something they have not been shown to be able to do.
  • New agent. Someone or something not previously in the causal system intervenes. This is the deus ex machina proper (§8.1), and it is the most reliably fatal of the three because it removes the protagonist from their own ending.

Test 4 — The inevitability-versus-predictability test

Could a reader have predicted this? Can a reader, now, imagine it otherwise?

Procedure. The test requires two readers, or one reader and some patience.

  • For predictability: stop a first-time reader thirty pages before the end and ask them to write, in two sentences, how the book will finish. Collect several such predictions.
  • For inevitability: ask a reader who has finished to describe an alternative ending they would have accepted. Ask them to make the case for it.

Interpretation. Map the results onto §6.2’s grid.

Predictions accurate? Alternative easy to construct? Diagnosis
No No — readers struggle to imagine another ending The target. Unforeseen and inevitable
Yes No Classical satisfaction. Not a defect; check that the pleasure is in the route (§6.2)
No Yes — readers produce alternatives readily and find them as good The ending is arbitrary. It is one of several available outcomes and the book has not distinguished it
Yes Yes The ending is inert. The book’s material never narrowed

The third row is the important one and the hardest to accept, because it is the result an ending gets when it is fine. An ending that could have gone several ways with equal justice has not concluded the book; it has stopped it in one of its possible positions.

Test 5 — The domain test

Which of the six domains does the ending deliver, versus merely gesture towards?

Procedure. Run the closural audit at §5.5. Then, for each domain marked present, apply the delivery discrimination: is there a specific passage a reader could point to, or is the domain being credited on the strength of the writer’s intention? Gesture is the commonest form of self-deception in an ending, because the writer supplies the missing content from their own knowledge.

Interpretation. Record which domains are delivered, which gestured, which refused, and which absent. Then ask, per §4.8, whether the distribution matches the book’s mode. A literary novel that delivers plot and gestures at character has probably mis-weighted; a thriller that delivers plot and refuses theme has probably not.

Test 6 — The proportion test

Is the aftermath the right size, and is each thread’s resolution proportional to the weight it carried?

Procedure. Two parts.

(a) The climax-to-end ratio. Measure the distance from the climax to the last word as a percentage of the book. Compare against §6.6’s table for the tradition being worked in. A figure outside the range is not a verdict but a question.

(b) The weight audit. List every subplot and major question. For each, record the approximate page space it occupied and the approximate page space its resolution receives. Chapter 14 (The Subplot) calls the first figure the attention tax; this is the refund.

Interpretation.

Pattern Diagnosis
Aftermath under 1% and four domains unserved §8.2, climax without aftermath
Aftermath over 10% in a contemporary literary novel Possibly §8.3 — but check the tradition first; it may be deliberate
Every thread resolved at roughly equal length §8.5, the tidy-everything ending. Uniform resolution destroys hierarchy
A thread that took 80 pages resolved in one sentence Under-delivery; the reader will experience this as the book losing interest in its own material
A thread that took 8 pages resolved in a chapter Over-delivery; usually a sign that the writer found this thread more interesting than the book did

The operational rule from §6.6: find the last decision any character takes and check how far the book runs past it. More than a page or two is usually consequence rather than ending.

Test 7 — The termination test

Has the book ended, or has it merely stopped?

Procedure. Herrnstein Smith’s distinction (§3.2), converted into a question a reader can answer. Give the last two pages to a reader who has read the book and ask a single question: “Is there anything after this?” Ask it neutrally. Then ask them to say what told them.

Interpretation. The value of the test is in the second answer. A reader who says “yes, it’s over” and can name what told them — a returned image, a shortened sentence, a settled grammatical form, a pattern completed — is reporting a closural device operating. A reader who says “yes” and cannot say why is reporting an effect that may be coming from the page or from the physical fact that the pages have run out, and the distinction matters: a book that closes only because the paper stops has terminated, not ended.

The reverse result is also informative and much more common than writers expect. A reader who says “no, I thought there was another chapter” has identified an ending that has not signalled itself, which is usually a formal problem (§4.6) rather than a content one and is often fixed by a terminal modification rather than by new material.

Test 8 — The tonal-match test

Does the final note match the tone the book has actually earned?

Procedure. Read the last page. Then read three passages from the middle third, chosen at random. Ask whether they belong to the same book — in register, in the narrator’s stance towards suffering, in what the prose finds important, and in what it permits itself to hope.

This is Chapter 9’s promise test (test 6 there) run in reverse, and the two chapters name the same violation at opposite ends of the book. Chapter 10 §8.2 calls the entrance version false advertising; this chapter calls the exit version the mismatched final note (§8.4). The map of this textbook (see §1.3) records the symmetry deliberately.

Interpretation. The characteristic failure is unearned uplift — a grim book closing on hope the material does not support — and its characteristic cause is not incompetence: the writer has spent two years inside a bleak book and wants, personally, to be let out. The inverse failure, the gratuitous darkening of a book that has earned warmth, is rarer and is usually an anxiety about seeming sentimental.

The refinement. Tone is not the same as outcome. A book may end in disaster and in a warm tone, or in success and in a cold one. What must match is the narrator’s relation to the material, not the material’s valence.

Test 9 — The reread test

Is a second reading a different and better experience — and does the ending survive being known?

Procedure. This test cannot be run quickly and is therefore the one most often omitted. Its cheap form: take three passages that the ending retroactively changes, and read them as a reader who knows how the book ends. Ask what they now yield.

Interpretation. Three outcomes.

  • The passages yield more. The ending has converted them into double-layered material — the reader who knows sees the preparation and the surface. This is the strongest available evidence of an earned ending, and it is the positive form of test 1.
  • The passages yield the same. Neutral. Much good writing does this.
  • The passages yield less. This is the significant result and it identifies a specific defect: an ending whose effect depended entirely on the reader not knowing. Strategy 13 (§6.4), the retrospective reframe, is the highest-risk case, and the standard for it is exacting: a novel that reframes must be better on a second reading, not merely different, or the reframe was a trick.

The test also catches a subtler problem. If the ending’s plants become obvious on rereading — if the seeded elements now look like seeds — the planting was too heavy (§6.2). The target is preparation that remains invisible as preparation even when the reader is looking for it.

Test 10 — The last-line test

Read the final paragraph in isolation. Does it carry weight given the full context — or does it coast on it?

Procedure. Copy the last paragraph onto a separate page. Read it cold. Then ask three questions:

  1. What is the reader looking at? (If the answer is “an abstraction”, the final image domain is unserved.)
  2. What does the last sentence do grammatically? (Assertion, question, fragment, subordinate clause; §4.5.)
  3. What is the last word? (Rabinowitz’s privileged position at maximum concentration.)

Interpretation. The important discrimination in this test separates it from Chapter 9’s voice test.

An ending is allowed to depend on everything that came before it. That is its condition, not its failure. Unlike an opening, which must work with nothing, a last line may be incomprehensible in isolation and perfect in place. For there she was means nothing on its own.

What it may not do is coast. A last line coasts when it borrows weight from the preceding book without adding any — when it is simply the next sentence, placed last, and could be exchanged for the sentence before it without loss.

The practical form: take the last paragraph and the paragraph before it, and ask whether the book could end on the earlier one. If it could, and nothing is lost, the final paragraph is not doing work. This is the ending’s version of Chapter 9 §8.7’s deletion ladder, and it has a similar hit rate.

7.2 Reading the results

Failed tests Likely diagnosis First move
2 and 3 Unearned in the strict sense; an element or agent is imported Plant it earlier — usually a sentence in the first third
4 (third row) alone The ending is arbitrary: one of several equally available outcomes The problem is in the middle of the book, not the end. Narrow the material
1 alone The ending strands earlier material Decide explicitly whether the ending or the scenes are wrong. Do not fix both
5 alone Domains mis-weighted for the mode Find one carrier that can take two of the unserved domains (§5.3)
6(a) low, 5 failing Climax without aftermath (§8.2) Add the lusis, not more climax
6(a) high Endless aftermath (§8.3) Find the last decision and end within a page of it
6(b) uniform Tidy-everything (§8.5) Cut resolutions, do not add them. Let two threads go unresolved and see what is lost
7 alone Termination without closure A formal device is missing: a terminal modification, a returned image, a settled last sentence
8 alone Mismatched final note Change the ending, not the book
9 alone The effect depended on ignorance If strategy 13, the reframe must add rather than replace. Otherwise reduce the surprise and increase the preparation
10 alone The last paragraph is not doing work Test whether the book ends a page earlier
Everything passes and the ending is flat §6.1’s Claim 2: preparation without pressure Nothing in this section will help. The middle of the book has not built a want the ending answers

A warning about the last row. It is the most common outcome of a competent revision, and the protocol cannot solve it, because the protocol audits the ending and the fault is elsewhere. An ending that satisfies every test and lands on nothing is reporting that the book was never about anything urgent enough to conclude. That is a real diagnosis, it is worth reaching, and it is why §16 argues that the last chapter is where a novel finds out what it was.
