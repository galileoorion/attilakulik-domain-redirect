14.1 Narratology and criticism

  • Bal, Mieke. Narratology: Introduction to the Theory of Narrative. 4th ed. Toronto: University of Toronto Press, 2017. [First edition 1985.]
  • Bakhtin, Mikhail. Problems of Dostoevsky’s Poetics. Translated by Caryl Emerson. Minneapolis: University of Minnesota Press, 1984.
  • Banfield, Ann. Unspeakable Sentences: Narration and Representation in the Language of Fiction. Boston: Routledge & Kegan Paul, 1982.
  • Booth, Wayne C. The Rhetoric of Fiction. 2nd ed. Chicago: University of Chicago Press, 1983. [First edition 1961.]
  • Booth, Wayne C. “Distance and Point-of-View: An Essay in Classification.” Essays in Criticism 11, no. 1 (1961): 60–79.
  • Bordwell, David. Narration in the Fiction Film. Madison: University of Wisconsin Press, 1985.
  • Chatman, Seymour. Story and Discourse: Narrative Structure in Fiction and Film. Ithaca: Cornell University Press, 1978.
  • Chatman, Seymour. Coming to Terms: The Rhetoric of Narrative in Fiction and Film. Ithaca: Cornell University Press, 1990. [Slant and filter.]
  • Cohn, Dorrit. Transparent Minds: Narrative Modes for Presenting Consciousness in Fiction. Princeton: Princeton University Press, 1978.
  • Forster, E. M. Aspects of the Novel. London: Edward Arnold, 1927.
  • Friedman, Norman. “Point of View in Fiction: The Development of a Critical Concept.” PMLA 70, no. 5 (1955): 1160–1184.
  • Genette, Gérard. Narrative Discourse: An Essay in Method. Translated by Jane E. Lewin. Ithaca: Cornell University Press, 1980. [French original 1972.]
  • James, Henry. The Art of the Novel: Critical Prefaces. Edited by R. P. Blackmur. New York: Scribner, 1934.
  • Lubbock, Percy. The Craft of Fiction. London: Jonathan Cape, 1921.
  • Rimmon-Kenan, Shlomith. Narrative Fiction: Contemporary Poetics. London: Methuen, 1983.
  • Spivak, Gayatri Chakravorty. “Three Women’s Texts and a Critique of Imperialism.” Critical Inquiry 12, no. 1 (1985): 243–261.
  • Stanzel, Franz K. A Theory of Narrative. Translated by Charlotte Goedsche. Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 1984.
  • Sternberg, Meir. Expositional Modes and Temporal Ordering in Fiction. Baltimore: Johns Hopkins University Press, 1978.
  • Uspensky, Boris. A Poetics of Composition. Translated by Valentina Zavarin and Susan Wittig. Berkeley: University of California Press, 1973.
  • Woloch, Alex. The One vs. the Many: Minor Characters and the Space of the Protagonist in the Realist Novel. Princeton: Princeton University Press, 2003.
  • Wood, James. How Fiction Works. London: Jonathan Cape, 2008.

14.2 Cognitive and empirical studies of reading

  • Duchan, Judith F., Gail A. Bruder, and Lynne E. Hewitt, eds. Deixis in Narrative: A Cognitive Science Perspective. Hillsdale, NJ: Lawrence Erlbaum, 1995.
  • Emmott, Catherine. Narrative Comprehension: A Discourse Perspective. Oxford: Oxford University Press, 1997.
  • Keen, Suzanne. Empathy and the Novel. Oxford: Oxford University Press, 2007.
  • Kidd, David Comer, and Emanuele Castano. “Reading Literary Fiction Improves Theory of Mind.” Science 342, no. 6156 (2013): 377–380.
  • Palmer, Alan. Fictional Minds. Lincoln: University of Nebraska Press, 2004.
  • Palmer, Alan. Social Minds in the Novel. Columbus: Ohio State University Press, 2010.
  • Panero, Maria Eugenia, et al. “Does Reading a Single Passage of Literary Fiction Really Improve Theory of Mind? An Attempt at Replication.” Journal of Personality and Social Psychology 111, no. 5 (2016): e46–e54.
  • Zunshine, Lisa. Why We Read Fiction: Theory of Mind and the Novel. Columbus: Ohio State University Press, 2006.
  • Zwaan, Rolf A., and Gabriel A. Radvansky. “Situation Models in Language Comprehension and Memory.” Psychological Bulletin 123, no. 2 (1998): 162–185.

  • Swain, Dwight V. Techniques of the Selling Writer. Norman: University of Oklahoma Press, 1965.

14.5 Added in the second edition

The Core chapter’s post-2000 and screen cases, each checked by a narrow lookup (recorded in the edition’s examples register):

  • Evaristo, Bernardine. Girl, Woman, Other. 2019. [Twelve women in four chapters of three; the after-party.]
  • Flynn, Gillian. Gone Girl. 2012. [Nick’s present-tense chapters and Amy’s diary; Part Two opens on Amy alive.]
  • Han Kang. The Vegetarian. 2007; trans. Deborah Smith, 2015. [Three parts, through the husband, the brother-in-law and the sister; Yeong-hye never holds a part.]
  • Moyes, Jojo. Me Before You. 2012. [Louisa Clark’s first person; a third-person prologue following Will before the accident; four chapters given to Camilla, Nathan, Steven and Katrina; Will never narrates.]
  • The Affair. Created by Sarah Treem and Hagai Levi. Showtime, 2014–19. [Season 1: each episode in two parts, Noah’s and Alison’s.]
  • “Long, Long Time.” The Last of Us, season 1, episode 3. Written by Craig Mazin, directed by Peter Hoar. HBO, 2023. [Bill and Frank across some twenty years; Joel and Ellie at the start and end.]
