Chapter 2 · Scene and Sequel

12. Studio Work

Continue in Core

12.1 Practical exercises

Exercise 1 — The beat annotation. Take three consecutive chapters of your draft and mark every beat in the margin: G, C, D, R, Di, De. Write 200 words on what the distribution shows.

Exercise 2 — The chain trace. Reverse-outline twenty consecutive units using only and then, but and therefore. Mark every “and then”. Rewrite the disaster preceding the first one so that it forces the goal that follows.

Exercise 3 — The beat-weight audit. Run the procedure at §5.5 over ten pairs and produce the plot of sequel length against disaster weight. Write 300 words interpreting it against the table there.

Exercise 4 — Compression ladder. Take one full sequel of about 600 words and rewrite it in 200 words, then 50 words, then one clause. Identify the point at which the chain becomes illegible (§5.4), and say what was carrying it at the last legible stage.

Exercise 5 — The costed dilemma. For a dilemma in your draft, write the two costs first, in one sentence each, without looking at the passage. Then rewrite the beat from the costs. Compare.

Exercise 6 — Structure substitution. Take a sequence that is not working and rewrite it as a different chain structure from §6.3 — a braided chain as a clean one, a clean chain with one deferred decision, a private sequel as a collective one. Do not judge until all versions exist.

Exercise 7 — The hinge hunt. Identify the one decision in your manuscript that your protagonist’s opening-chapter self would not have made. Write 400 words on what changed and where. If you cannot find one, that is the exercise’s result.

Exercise 8 — Displacement. Take a disaster that happens to your protagonist and give the reaction beat to another character, in another scene. Assess what the event gains and loses (§6.3, structure 6).

Exercise 9 — The published pair. Choose any twenty pages of a published novel and produce a full beat analysis, including compression ratios. Write 600 words on what the analysis cannot capture.

12.2 Seminar questions

  1. Does Crime and Punishment break the model or complete it? §9.1 argues that the novel runs one iteration of the six beats across six hundred pages. Argue the opposite case: that a “sequel” of five parts is a different kind of object, and that calling it a sequel is a category error that saves the model by emptying it.

  2. Is the Hinge a beat, a property, or a category error? Argue for or against admitting the criterion change as a distinct unit (§4.7), using Jane Eyre, Crime and Punishment and one novel in which no criterion ever changes.

  3. Is the prohibition on the passive protagonist a structural principle or a genre preference? §8.7 concedes that it is close to the latter. Test the claim against The Stranger, Oblomov and The Remains of the Day, and consider what value system the prohibition encodes.

  4. What replaces causality in Catch-22? §9.6 argues that Heller substitutes an escalating quota and a backwards-running disclosure. Is that enough, or does the novel simply exchange one chain for another and thereby concede the principle it attacks?

  5. Do the intercalary chapters of The Grapes of Wrath earn their place? Some readers find them interruptive. Is the collective sequel a structural achievement or a rhetorical intrusion — and does the answer depend on the reader or on the book?

  6. Whose chain? The model was codified in a mid-century American commercial market (§3.6). Examine a narrative tradition organised on other principles — the frame-tale sequence, the episodic picaresque, the modernist novel of a single day — and say which of this chapter’s prohibitions describe narrative and which describe a local literary culture.

12.3 Assessment rubric

The following rubric is for a submitted scene–sequel pair (1,500–2,000 words) with a 750-word critical commentary.

Band Craft (60%) Commentary (40%)
Distinction Goal legible and descended; resistance that alters the goal’s shape; a disaster with a nameable currency; a reaction carried by body and attention; a dilemma whose costs a reader could weigh either way; a decision that forecloses. Beat weights deliberately set Names its chain structure and defends it against alternatives; identifies its own unsolved problems; engages at least two theoretical sources critically
Merit All beats present; the chain holds; one beat under-delivered or mis-weighted Accurate self-analysis; theory applied rather than cited; risks acknowledged
Pass A pair exists; goal and disaster present; sequel thin or summarised Describes the work accurately; theory referenced but not applied
Fail No disaster, or no sequel; the chain cannot be traced Description without analysis; failure patterns unrecognised
