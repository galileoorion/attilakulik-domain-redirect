Chapter 3 · The Dialogue Exchange

9. Checklist and Exercises

Continue in Reference

Revision checklist

Run each exchange of a finished draft against this list. Each item corresponds to one of the diagnostic tests or failure patterns; an unticked item is a question to answer, not a verdict.

Engine

Cost and status

Two channels

Voices

Apparatus and room

Exercises

1. The want sentence. Choose the longest exchange in your draft. Above it, write what each speaker wants from the other before it ends, one sentence each, with a verb aimed at the other person. If one sentence will not come, rewrite the exchange until it does, or summarise it.

2. Four mechanisms. Take the most direct line in one exchange and rewrite it four times: as an evasion, a changed subject, a too-precise reply and a delayed agreement. Keep the version that tells you something about the speaker you had not decided.

3. The transcript test. Strip every tag and action beat from two pages of dialogue, and give the bare lines to a reader who does not know the book. Ask her to mark where the speaker changes and to describe each speaker in two adjectives.

4. The domain audit. Beside each turn of one exchange, write the letters of the domains it serves: P, C, R, Th, Tx, S. Most turns carrying P alone means a briefing; R never appearing means status is fixed; S never appearing means everyone says what they think; one turn carrying most of the letters is the exchange’s spine.

5. Find the plain line. List the three places in your book where a character says plainly what she means about what matters most to her. If there are more than three, choose the one the book is building towards and restore the gap in the others.
