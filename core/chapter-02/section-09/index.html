Chapter 2 · Scene and Sequel

9. Checklist and Exercises

Revision checklist

Run each scene–sequel pair in a finished draft against this list. Each item corresponds to one of the diagnostic tests or failure patterns; an unticked item is a question to answer, not a verdict.

Exercises

1. The three-connective outline. Outline one chapter of your draft as a list of events joined only by and then, but and therefore. Mark every and then, and rewrite the disaster before the first one so that it forces the next goal.

2. Costs before options. Take the hardest choice your protagonist makes. Before rereading it, write what each option costs, one sentence each. Then rewrite the dilemma from the costs, giving the option your character refuses its best case.

3. The beat-weight audit. Run the audit in the diagnostic tests over ten consecutive pairs from the middle of your draft. Find the largest disaster and the longest sequel. If they are not the same pair, decide which one is wrong.

4. Compress and expand. Rewrite one of your full sequels as a single sentence that carries reaction, dilemma and decision. Then expand one of your compressed sequels to a page. Read both against their disasters and decide which setting each pair needs.

5. Find the hinge. List your protagonist’s five largest decisions and, beside each, the rule by which the character chose. If the rule never changes, either find the decision that should be made on new grounds or write a paragraph explaining why your book, like Manchester by the Sea, is about a criterion that cannot move.
