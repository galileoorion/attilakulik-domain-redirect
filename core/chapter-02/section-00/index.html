Chapter 2 · Scene and Sequel

Scene and Sequel

Continue in Reference

How one unit of a novel produces the next

The Craft Behind the Story (Second Edition) — Chapter 2. Part I: The Scene and Its Materials.

In brief. A scene is held in place by what comes before and after it. This chapter divides the unit of a novel in two: a scene, in which a character pursues a concrete goal, meets resistance and ends worse off; and a sequel, in which the character absorbs the setback, weighs options that all cost something, and decides. The decision becomes the next scene’s goal. Where those two links hold, each event reads as something that had to happen next; where either breaks, the book reads as a list. The tools are six named beats, a principle that page-space should follow the weight of what happened, a test for whether a character’s grounds for choosing ever change, and ten diagnostic questions. Use them on a finished draft, on a run of pages that feels busy but inert, or beautiful but static. They describe one way of building momentum, and a book that runs on another should know that it does.

What you can do after this chapter

  1. Find the six beats in a passage, including the ones compressed into a clause or a verb.
  2. Trace a chain back three links and locate the point at which “because” has turned into “and then”.
  3. Price a dilemma by writing both costs before writing the options.
  4. Check whether the space each sequel gets matches the weight of the setback it follows.
  5. Find the decision at which your protagonist chooses on new grounds, or learn that there is none.

Contents

The first files set out the model, the middle ones show how it runs, and the last are for use at the desk.

  1. The Problem
    Two opposite drafts with one complaint, a household paragraph, and the chapter’s claim
  2. Definitions
    Scene and sequel in the narrow sense, the senses of “scene”, adjacent terms
  3. How It Works
    Causation, desire and delay, the reader’s search for causes, and where the model comes from
  4. The Taxonomy
    The six beats, each with its carriers, its failures and its compressed form
  5. The Mechanism
    The two links, compression, deferral and the hinge
  6. Diagnostic Tests
    Ten questions on one page, and the beat-weight audit
  7. Failure Patterns
    Five recurrent failures, each with a published work that commits it and succeeds
  8. Worked Analyses
    Crime and Punishment, Small Things Like These, The Martian, Mad Max: Fury Road, and the household revision case
  9. Checklist and Exercises
    The revision checklist and five exercises for your own draft
  10. Coda
    What the model can and cannot tell a writer

A writer already in revision can start at the diagnostic tests and use the checklist as the working page.

The full treatment

The Reference layer holds the complete chapter, Scene and Sequel: the theory in full (Propp’s functions, Todorov’s equilibrium schema, Burke’s pentad, situation models), each beat’s carriers and failures in tabular form, twelve chain structures, twelve failure patterns, eight close readings including Jane Eyre, Great Expectations, Dune and Catch-22, scene and sequel in the short story, the series, screen and translation, seminar questions and an assessment rubric, and the sources.

See also: The Purposes of a Scene (Chapter 1), for what a single unit is for; The Chapter Break (Chapter 6), for where a break falls on a deferred decision.
