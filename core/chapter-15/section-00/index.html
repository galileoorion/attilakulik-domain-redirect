Chapter 15 · The Reveal

The Reveal

Continue in Reference

What the reader learns late, and what it must change

The Craft Behind the Story (Second Edition) — Chapter 15. Part III: The Architecture of the Whole.

In brief. A reveal is a late disclosure that changes what the reader understands of pages already read. It is earned when those pages bear two readings, the one the reader made and the one the disclosure makes, and when something inside the book, a character who does not know or a teller with a reason not to say, explains why the truth came when it did. It may reinterpret anything the double reading reaches, and falsify only what a teller in the book could have shaped. The chapter’s tools are the double-reading table, which sets each passage bearing on a reveal against its first and second readings, and the disclosure map, which records who learns each fact when: the reader and each character. Use the chapter when readers guess a twist early, feel cheated by it, or shrug at it, and when a book holds a secret and the writer does not know where to spend it.

What you can do after this chapter

  1. Test whether the pages before a reveal support both readings, and find the passages that give it away or tell a lie.
  2. Name who withholds each truth from the reader, and why, and repair a withholding that has no reason in the book.
  3. Decide when the reader should learn a fact relative to the characters, and what question the reveal should leave in place of the one it answers.
  4. Judge how far a late disclosure may recast a whole book, and when a book may decline to reveal at all.
  5. Diagnose a reveal that cheats, gives itself away, arrives by report or changes nothing, and revise it.

Contents

The first files say what a reveal is and what readers do with one, the central files how a reveal is prepared, withheld, timed and spent, and the last are for use at the desk.

  1. The Problem
    A household convergence at which the book’s largest truth is spent in two sentences of dialogue, and the chapter’s claim
  2. Definitions
    Reveal, double reading and the terms around them, and what other chapters keep
  3. How It Works
    What a reader does at a reveal, what she feels before one, what makes one fair, how far a teller can be trusted, and what the evidence says
  4. The Taxonomy
    Four objects of a reveal, three positions, and two rulings: the declined reveal and the teller rule
  5. The Mechanism
    How a double reading is built, who may withhold, how disclosure is timed, and how several reveals share a book
  6. Diagnostic Tests
    Eight questions on one page, and how to read their failures together
  7. Failure Patterns
    Five recurrent failures, each with a work that commits it and succeeds
  8. Worked Analyses
    Atonement, Gone Girl, We Are All Completely Beside Ourselves, The Sixth Sense and the household revision case
  9. Checklist and Exercises
    The revision checklist and five exercises for your own draft
  10. Coda
    What the model assumes, and why a reveal is a debt the book has been paying all along

A writer in revision can start at the diagnostic tests, with a double-reading table drafted for the book’s largest reveal. A writer planning a book with a secret should read the mechanism first, because where a truth is withheld, and by whom, is easier to design than to retrofit.

The full treatment

This is a new chapter, so the Reference layer holds a single full treatment rather than a corrected original: the theory at greater length, an extended catalogue of reveals and failure patterns, the procedures for each test, further analyses (The Murder of Roger Ackroyd, The Silent Patient and Knives Out), the household double-reading table and disclosure map in full, a glossary of the chapter’s terms and the sources.

See also: Backstory and Flashback (Chapter 7), for the mechanics of returning to the past once a reveal has made it necessary; The Viewpoint Shift (Chapter 13), whose knowledge boundary is the commonest reason a truth is withheld; The Ending (Chapter 16), where a closing reveal falls due and the plant audit checks its preparation; Non-linear and Multi-timeline Architecture (Chapter 8), for truths withheld by the arrangement of timelines.
