Introduction

3. The Household Novel

Continue in Reference

One invented book

Every chapter revises the same invented novel, so that a reader can watch the book’s instruments work on one body of material and see each chapter’s repair take its place among the others. The novel is a quiet contemporary novel of family life, British in setting and register, and its subject is what a family withholds from itself.

A father kept the ledger of a small shop for eleven years, in a careful slanting hand with crossed sevens. He used a fountain pen, the good pen, for the ledger only. He was ill, wrote the illness into the ledger, with dates and a doctor’s name, and told nobody. He died in March. After his death the mother changed the shop’s locks, and she never says why. Four months later his three adult children are working out what happened from the objects he left. Elena, who drives a truck and has had a key to the shop since she was twelve, learns in her brother’s doorway that the key no longer fits, and wants to know why. Nadia, who counts things, watches the dust gather on her father’s coat on its hook in the flat above the shop and has learned not to touch it. Dov, the brother, gave Elena the new key and kept the reason to himself; he has been writing in the ledger since the evening of 9 March, when his father’s last entry broke off, and he is selling the shop without telling his sisters.

The book opens with a prologue on the father’s last ordinary day, on which he pockets the good pen and cannot say why. Its main narrative is told in close third person and divided between Elena and Nadia. Dov is seen only from outside, except in one bounded chapter, the evening of 9 March, near the climax. The mother is never given a viewpoint. The book ends with the three siblings reading the ledger together in the shop, and with Nadia going up to the flat and putting on the coat.

How the chapters use it

Each chapter’s household case works the same way. A first draft of some passage, called Version A, fails the chapter’s tests in a way the chapter can diagnose; a revision, Version B, repairs it; and the case ends by naming what the revision has not solved. The revisions are consistent with one another because a single record of the book’s facts governs them: who knows what when, what each object looks like and where it lives, and in what order the scenes occur. Appendix D summarises that record.

The cases accumulate. The descriptive-detail chapter plants the ledger as an unemphatic object near the coat; the chapter-break chapter has Elena find it and see that the hand changes at 9 March; the viewpoint chapter gives Dov his one chapter and shows whose the second hand is; the ending pays the plants off. The chapters new to this edition revise the book further. The middle chapter places the scenes between the discovery of the ledger and Dov’s chapter; the arc chapter gives Elena a decision at the convergence; the reveal chapter decides when the father’s illness reaches the reader; the multi-timeline chapter charts the book’s two times; the genre chapter decides what the book promises; and the structural-edit chapter takes a set of readers’ notes on the whole draft and sorts them by where their causes lie. Each of these later revisions is offered as a proposal, argued for and left, as an editor’s proposal is left, for the author to accept or decline.

The ending audits the whole: every element the ending relies on is traced back to the chapter that planted it, to show that nothing load-bearing arrives for the first time on the last pages. One element is unresolved by design. The mother’s reason for changing the locks is never given, and the book’s later chapters treat that silence as a question the book visibly declines rather than one it forgets. A novel that closed every account would argue that families can be solved, which this one does not believe; a different novelist would write the other book, and the chapters say what that choice costs.

See also: Appendix D, with the people, objects, timeline, scene order and the full table of plants and payoffs; The Ending (Chapter 16), where the plant audit is run.
