Chapter 18 · The Structural Edit

4. The Taxonomy

Continue in Reference

The revision ladder has five rungs. Each holds decisions, the instruments from earlier chapters that test them, and the symptoms readers report when they fail. A decision on one rung can undo decisions below it, but leaves those above it intact. The order applies to the kinds of novels this book models; The Mechanism explains where it bends.

Rung 1: the promise

The first rung holds the promise: what kind of book this is, what question its choices answer, and what it declines to answer. Series and Genre (Chapter 17) tests the genre’s core promise, whether the book keeps, varies or declines it, and whether the book stands alone or belongs to a series. Arc and Theme (Chapter 12) uses an answer map to test the governing thematic question and each major character’s answer. The Opening Pages (Chapter 9) tests the contracts the opening makes; at this rung, they are decisions about what the book owes. The instruments are the promise inventory and the answer map. A writer should be able to state the rung in two sentences: what the reader is promised, and what question the book’s choices answer.

A change on this rung reopens everything below it, which is why it is checked first and changed reluctantly. It fails in symptoms that readers seldom locate: an ending that was “not what the book promised”, a book that “didn’t know what it wanted to be”, and, less obviously, a middle that sags because nothing in the book’s question makes one outcome more urgent than another.

Rung 2: allocation and arrangement

The second rung holds allocation and arrangement: whose book it is, how many stories it tells, and how it orders time and takes form. The Viewpoint Shift (Chapter 13) tests which minds receive a viewpoint, for how long and from when. The Subplot (Chapter 14) tests the threads and their convergence. Non-linear and Multi-timeline Architecture (Chapter 8) tests the arrangement of times with its order chart and strand table. The Prologue (Chapter 10) tests whether the book needs a prologue. The rung also holds decisions about form: the person and tense of the narration, the book’s divisions, and any frame, apparatus or repeated pattern it declares. Its instruments are the admission test, convergence test, order chart, strand table and form audit described later in this chapter.

A change on this rung moves whole stretches of the book, so it reopens every rung below for the units it moves. Its symptoms are the reader who could not tell the viewpoints apart, who preferred the subplot, who skimmed one timeline or did not know when she was, or who took the prologue for a different book.

Rung 3: the shape

The third rung holds the shape of the whole: where the protagonist’s change occurs, how the middle narrows, when each fact is disclosed, and what the ending spends. Arc and Theme (Chapter 12) uses a criterion log to locate the decision where the character’s rule for choosing changes. The Middle (Chapter 11) uses an option list to test how the middle narrows and a value-shift log to check for repetition. The Reveal (Chapter 15) maps when facts reach the reader and uses a double-reading table to test their effect. The Ending (Chapter 16) tests the ending with its plant audit and last-decision rule. These four parts share a rung because they affect one another: moving a reveal changes what the middle narrows, and moving the arc’s decision changes what the ending must pay. Check them together in the order middle, arc, reveal, ending, since the ending depends on all three.

A change on this rung reopens the joins and scenes near the decisions it moves. Its symptoms are the classic reports on a draft: the middle drags, the protagonist does not change or changes from nowhere, the twist was guessed or felt like a cheat, the ending fell flat or was too tidy.

Rung 4: the joins

The fourth rung holds the joins, where one unit of the book meets the next. The Chapter Break (Chapter 6) asks what each chapter proposes and how its ending prices stopping. The Transition (Chapter 5) counts what changes across a gap. Backstory and Flashback (Chapter 7) checks how a cutaway into the past is compressed, what triggers it and how the narrative returns. The Opening Pages (Chapter 9) tests the contracts the opening makes on the page. The opening joins the reader to the book, so revise it after making every decision it must promise.

A change on this rung reopens the openings and closings of the scenes on either side. Its symptoms are local but global in effect: a book that readers call relentless or slow when no scene is at fault, a reader who stalls after scene breaks, flashbacks skimmed, a first chapter put down.

Rung 5: the scene and its materials

The last rung holds the scene and its materials. The Purposes of a Scene (Chapter 1) tests what each scene changes with a two-sentence statement and five registers. Scene and Sequel (Chapter 2) tests how scene and sequel link. The Dialogue Exchange (Chapter 3) asks what each exchange does; The Descriptive Detail (Chapter 4) asks which details are named. The line edit follows this rung. A change here reopens nothing above it. Sentences come last because every higher decision can disturb them, while revising them need not disturb any higher decision.

The ladder in one table

The table gathers the five rungs with their instruments and the symptoms that most often bring a writer to each. It is a summary of the paragraphs above, not a substitute for the chapters it names.

Rung Decisions Instruments Typical symptoms
1 Kind of book, governing question, what is declined, standalone or series Promise inventory (Series and Genre, Chapter 17), answer map (Arc and Theme, Chapter 12), contracts as decisions (The Opening Pages, Chapter 9) “Not what it promised”; “didn’t know what it wanted to be”
2 Viewpoint grant, threads, order of times, prologue, form Admission test (The Viewpoint Shift, Chapter 13), convergence test (The Subplot, Chapter 14), order chart and strand table (Non-linear and Multi-timeline Architecture, Chapter 8), double test (The Prologue, Chapter 10), form audit (The Structural Edit, Chapter 18) Viewpoints indistinct; subplot preferred; lost in time; prologue resented
3 Arc, middle, reveals, ending Criterion log (Arc and Theme, Chapter 12), option list (The Middle, Chapter 11), disclosure map (The Reveal, Chapter 15), plant audit (The Ending, Chapter 16) Middle drags; no change; twist guessed; ending flat
4 Chapter breaks, transitions, cutaways, opening pages Ending types (The Chapter Break, Chapter 6), count of change (The Transition, Chapter 5), compression check (Backstory and Flashback, Chapter 7), contracts as written (The Opening Pages, Chapter 9) Relentless or slow; stalls at breaks; flashbacks skimmed; first chapter put down
5 Scenes, sequels, exchanges, details Two-sentence statement (The Purposes of a Scene, Chapter 1), the six beats (Scene and Sequel, Chapter 2), the domains of the exchange (The Dialogue Exchange, Chapter 3) and of the detail (The Descriptive Detail, Chapter 4) Nothing happens; flat talk; description that tells nothing

Read across a row, the table says where to look; read down a column, it shows how much of the book each rung governs, which is the reason for the order. The symptoms column is a first guess, since a symptom felt on one rung is often caused on a higher one.

Proposals from earlier chapters

The Ending (Chapter 16) proposed formal closure as a sixth domain of the ending. Formal closure completes, modifies or disrupts a book’s structural pattern rather than its story: a frame returns, for example, or an apparatus follows the narrative. The chapter adopted the domain for diagnosis but conceded an objection: formal closure may serve the other domains rather than give the reader something of its own. It left this chapter to decide, using Nineteen Eighty-Four’s Appendix and the last episode of Six Feet Under as test cases.

This chapter rejects formal closure as a domain of the ending and adopts it at the scale of the book as the form audit, a test on rung 2: for every formal pattern a book declares, its person and tense, its divisions, any frame, apparatus or repeated shape, the audit asks whether the pattern is completed, modified for a reason the book shows, or removed from the place where it was declared. Both test cases pass the audit, and both show why the domain belongs there. In the first chapter of Nineteen Eighty-Four a footnote declares the apparatus: For an account of its structure and etymology see Appendix. The Appendix discharges it. Its past tense can be read as looking back on Newspeak from a later time, an interpretation that offers a hope the story itself denies, though the text does not settle the Appendix’s narrator or date. Six Feet Under opened nearly every episode on a death, and its last episode, which opens instead on a birth, ends on a montage of the principal characters’ deaths decades ahead; the pattern is completed and modified at its last instance, and what the viewer receives is the series’ theme, each life shown whole. In both, the formal pattern is the carrier and the payment lies in another domain, so the objection holds at the ending. The Ending’s defence (Chapter 16), that formal closure has distinct carriers, failures and remedies, holds for the audit: a frame opened and never closed is a decision on rung 2 that the ending has failed to discharge, and the remedy is as often to remove the frame from the opening as to close it at the end.
