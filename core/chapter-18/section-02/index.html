Chapter 18 · The Structural Edit

2. Definitions

Continue in Reference

Working definition

A structural edit revises the decisions that span more than one scene in a complete draft. It asks what the book promises and what question it poses, whose book it is, and how its times and threads are arranged. It then examines the shape of the whole: the protagonist’s change, the narrowing of the middle, the schedule of disclosure and the ending. Finally, it examines the joins between the book’s units. The writer or editor attends to the book as a whole and reads the draft as a set of decisions rather than a sequence of pages. A structural edit may require a rewrite, in which a scene is drafted again from a statement of what it must do, a revision made in place, or both.

Two later kinds of work follow the structural edit. A line edit revises sentences and paragraphs inside a scene for sense, rhythm, economy and voice; it takes the scene’s job as given. A copy-edit checks spelling, grammar, house style and facts such as names, dates and the colour of a coat for correctness and consistency. Publishers’ editors also call the structural edit a developmental edit. Their sequence, structure, then line, then copy, follows dependence: there is no point correcting a paragraph’s spelling if the paragraph will be cut. A writer working alone does all three jobs and may be tempted to do them at once, though the same order serves her.

Two further terms come from the chapter’s own instruments. The book map is a table with one row per scene. Each row records the scene’s place in the draft, its time and setting, its viewpoint and thread, what changes, and what the scene deposits for later. The revision ladder orders the book’s decisions from those on which other decisions rest down to those that depend on everything else. Each level is a rung. The Mechanism explains the map, and The Taxonomy sets out the ladder.

Adjacent terms

The order of dependence is the relation the ladder records. If changing one decision can make another wrong, but the reverse change leaves the first intact, the second decision depends on the first. A chapter break depends on the arrangement of the book’s times: moving a strand may leave the break far from the question it once opened. Moving the break, however, leaves the strands where they were. Dependence belongs to decisions, not to the order of pages. The opening pages come first in the book but depend on almost everything after them.

Planning and editing use the same order. A writer who outlines before drafting makes the decisions at the top of the ladder first and drafts the scenes last, and a writer who drafts first and discovers the book in the drafting makes the same decisions afterwards, in the edit. The ladder serves both, as an order of decisions rather than of drafts.

The plant audit of The Ending (Chapter 16) traces everything an ending rests on to the place it was planted. It is a test of the ending against the book, and the structural edit uses it as the last test of the shape of the whole. The chapter’s other neighbours are the diagnostic tests of every earlier chapter, each of which tests the text at one level; this chapter says in what order to run them and what to do with what they find.

What this chapter leaves to others

This chapter adds no test of the text. Every test it orders belongs to the chapter that defines it, and the instruments it reads off the book map (the order chart, the option list, the criterion log, the disclosure map, the promise inventory and the rest) are defined and justified in their own chapters. The tests in this chapter are tests of the edit: whether it is being done in an order that will hold, whether each repair has been checked against what it disturbs, and whether it is finished.

The line edit and the copy-edit are outside the book’s subject, which is structure. The consolidated symptom index and the master checklist, which gather every chapter’s symptoms and checklists in one place, are in the appendices; this chapter gives a short table from symptom to rung, enough to start an edit, and leaves the full index to them. The working relation between a writer and an editor, and the questions it raises about whose book an edited book is, are touched on in the full treatment, section 8, and not otherwise treated.
