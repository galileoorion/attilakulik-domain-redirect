Chapter 18 · The Structural Edit

1. The Problem

Continue in Reference

The household novel is drafted from end to end. It is a quiet family novel set in a small British town: four months after their father’s death in March, his three adult children reconstruct what he left. Elena learns in Dov’s doorway that their mother changed the shop’s locks, and Dov gives her the new key and keeps the reason; Nadia counts things and will not touch the coat that still hangs in the flat above the shop, its shoulders brushed every week by someone the book does not name; the father’s ledger, kept for eleven years in a careful slanting hand, stops on 9 March, and a looser hand continues it. Dov’s single chapter of viewpoint, near the end, is the evening of 9 March. At the convergence the three read the ledger together, learn that their father was ill and wrote it down and told nobody, and Nadia goes up to the flat and puts on the coat.

Three early readers and the agent have sent notes, eleven in all. The opening is slow, and it takes a chapter to learn whose book it is. One reader kept waiting to learn who brushes the coat. The middle sags somewhere between Nadia finding the pen and Dov’s chapter. At the head of Dov’s chapter one reader did not know when she was. The ending is crowded, with too much happening in the shop at once. Elena does not seem to change. The ledger is warm three times. And there are four notes about sentences.

The writer does what most writers do with notes: she opens the file at page one and works forward. She tightens the opening and adds a sentence telling the reader that the book is also Elena’s. At about 45 per cent she writes a short scene in which Nadia, coming up to the flat early, finds their mother there with a clothes brush, and leaves without a word. Where the middle sags she adds a chapter in which Elena searches the flat. She puts a paragraph at the head of Dov’s chapter explaining that this is the evening of 9 March, four months earlier. At the convergence she cuts Elena’s call to Nadia, to thin the crowd, and has Nadia arrive by chance; and since a reader said Elena does not change, she gives her a paragraph of reflection on the last pages. She takes warm out twice.

Every one of these repairs answers its note, and the book is worse. The scene with the clothes brush gives the mother a scene of her own, which the book has refused her from its first chapter, and answers in passing a question the book was built to decline. The search chapter repeats the empty drawer at the same stakes, which is how middles sag in the first place. The paragraph at the head of Dov’s chapter states what a heading could show. Cutting the call removes the one act in which Elena’s change is visible, and the paragraph of reflection then has to say what the act had shown. And the opening the writer polished first now promises a book whose middle and ending she has since changed. Six weeks of careful work have produced a draft whose parts no longer agree, and the notes that remain are harder to read than the first ones, because some of them are about the repairs.

The fault is not in any single repair. It is in the order. A novel’s decisions depend on one another, and they depend in one direction: the arrangement of a book’s times depends on what the book is asking, the chapter breaks depend on the arrangement, and the sentences depend on everything. A writer who revises in page order makes each repair against decisions she has not yet checked, and repairs at the top of the book undo work done lower down.

A structural edit revises a book in the order in which its decisions depend on one another, not in the order of its pages: first what the book promises and asks, then whose it is and how its times and threads are arranged, then the shape of the whole, then the joins, and the scenes and their sentences last, because a change at any level reopens work beneath it and none above. Diagnosis runs the other way, from the place where a reader felt a problem to the highest level at which its cause can lie, and the edit is finished when a pass at the top changes nothing below and every failure that remains is one the writer can name and defend.

The claim has a consequence for notes. A reader’s note says where a problem was felt, which is evidence, and seldom where it was caused. The revision in Worked Analyses takes the same eleven notes, sorts them by the level at which each cause lies, mends four of them, keeps two failures it can defend, passes the other five to the line edit, and finds that the note about the coat, a note about a detail, is answered by a decision about what the book declines.
