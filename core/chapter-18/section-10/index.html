Chapter 18 · The Structural Edit

10. Coda

Continue in Reference

The revision ladder assumes that a novel is a set of decisions, that some of them rest on others, and that the dependence runs mostly one way, from what a book is for, through how it is arranged and shaped, to the joins and the sentences. The book map assumes that a book can be seen whole at the size of a table, and that what readers build as they read, a world indexed by time, place, person, cause and goal, is what a writer must be able to see. Neither describes how a book is written. Books are discovered in the drafting, often from the bottom, and the order of dependence says only what to do with a discovery once it is made: take it to the rung it belongs to, decide there, and come down again.

That is the chapter’s argument, and it is the argument of the book’s earlier chapters taken together. Each of them described a unit, from the detail to the ending, and gave a test for whether it works; this chapter has added no test of the text, only an order in which the tests are worth running, since a test passed on a rung whose decisions above are unsettled can be failed again by the next repair. The order explains why careful revision so often makes a book worse, and why the cure is seldom more care. A repair is good only relative to the decisions it rests on, so the quality of an edit depends as much on its sequence as on its judgements.

What the ladder cannot do is decide its own top rung. What a book promises and what question it asks are decisions, not diagnoses, and no test in this book makes them; the tests can only show that a decision has not been made, or has been made twice. The household revision shows the limit: its most consequential repair was a decision about what the book declines, and the chapter could propose it but not prove it, which is why it is left, with the book’s other proposals, for the author to confirm.

See also: the full treatment, section 8, on the limits of the model; The Ending (Chapter 16), whose plant audit is the last test of the shape; Series and Genre (Chapter 17), whose promise inventory is the first.
