Chapter 9 · The Opening Pages

The Opening Pages

Continue in Reference

What the first pages of the main narrative owe the reader, and how to tell whether they pay it

The Craft Behind the Story (Second Edition) — Chapter 9. Part III: The Architecture of the Whole.

In brief. The opening pages are the first stretch of a novel’s main narrative, and they are the only pages that must work as an ordinary scene while teaching a reader who has invested nothing what kind of book she has entered. They cannot draw on trust earned earlier, because nothing came earlier. The chapter’s tool is the contract model: an opening opens obligations of voice, genre, character, world and stakes, and, as proposed here, of form, which the rest of the book must keep. A page cannot give each obligation its own sentence, so the work is done by selection, by finding the details that already carry several obligations at once. Ten diagnostic questions turn the model into a revision order. Use the chapter on a first chapter that readers put down, on one that hooks and is then betrayed by its own book, or on one that holds all the necessary information and none of the pull.

What you can do after this chapter

  1. Mark which obligations each sentence of a first page opens, and say whether each one left for later was deferred by choice.
  2. Find the detail in a draft that already carries several obligations, and build the page outward from it.
  3. Decide where in the story the telling begins, and find the small deviation that answers why today?
  4. Tell an opening that hooks as a by-product of its work from one that has borrowed its hook from a more exciting book.
  5. Run ten tests on a draft opening and turn their failures into a revision order.

Key terms

Opening pages; the threshold problem and the zero-context condition; the hook; the attack; exposition and orientation; paratext; the rules of notice and the primacy effect; the situation model and the contextual frame; the horizon of expectations; suspense and curiosity; the contract model, with the voice, genre, character, world and stakes contracts and the proposed formal contract; narrative distance and free indirect style; reliability and the reading protocol; normalisation; the immediate want; the pull-forward question; planting and withholding; multiplicity, selection and the density ceiling; in medias res and the hybrid resolution; the hairline crack; the opening gambits and substitution testing; the multiplicity audit. Each is defined at first use and collected in the consolidated glossary.

Contents

The first files say what the opening is and why it carries the weight it does, the middle files what it owes and how the debt is paid, and the last are for use at the desk.

  1. The Problem
    A book’s best scene moved to page one, and the chapter’s claim
  2. Definitions
    The opening pages, how long they last, the hook, adjacent terms
  3. How It Works
    The rules of notice and primacy, the situation model, the reader’s horizon and the paratext, the distribution of exposition, and the dates of the convention
  4. The Taxonomy
    Five contracts, and a proposed sixth: the formal contract
  5. The Mechanism
    Multiplicity as selection, the density ceiling, the attack, the hairline crack and the gambits
  6. Diagnostic Tests
    Ten questions on one page, and how to read their failures together
  7. Failure Patterns
    Five recurrent failures, each with a published work that commits it and succeeds
  8. Worked Analyses
    Pride and Prejudice, Everything I Never Told You, Lincoln in the Bardo, Inglourious Basterds and The Sopranos, and the household revision case
  9. Checklist and Exercises
    The revision checklist and five exercises for your own draft
  10. Coda
    Why the opening is revised last

A writer in revision can start at the diagnostic tests and the multiplicity audit among the exercises; the coda, which says when the tests mislead, is best read early.

The full treatment

The Reference layer holds the complete chapter, The Opening Pages: the theory at length, each contract’s components and carriers with further illustrations, twelve opening gambits with their gains and costs, ten failure patterns, seven close readings (among them The Stranger in three translations), openings in the short story, the novella, braided books and series, a concordance of concepts to novels, seminar questions, an assessment rubric and the sources.

See also: The Prologue (Chapter 10), on the separated unit that may come before the opening; The Ending (Chapter 16), the formal inverse, read against everything instead of nothing; The Chapter Break (Chapter 6), on later chapter openings, where the reader arrives already oriented; Series and Genre (Chapter 17), on the opening of a series instalment.
