Chapter 16 · The Ending

The Ending

Continue in Reference

What the last pages may spend, and what they owe

The Craft Behind the Story (Second Edition) — Chapter 16. Part III: The Architecture of the Whole.

In brief. The ending is the last stretch of a novel, its climax and whatever aftermath the book needs, and it differs from every other unit in two ways: nothing follows it to correct what it does, and the reader rereads the whole book through it. A weak ending therefore damages the book, not only its last pages, and an ending may spend only what the book has already deposited. The chapter’s tools are the plant audit, which traces everything the ending rests on back to the place it was planted, and the last-decision rule, which ends the aftermath within a page of the last decision anyone takes. Use the chapter when an ending feels unearned, flat, tidy, abrupt or overlong, or when you cannot tell whether a deliberate openness reads as a choice or as an evasion.

What you can do after this chapter

  1. Separate the climax from the aftermath, and resolution from closure, and say which one a draft lacks.
  2. Name the domains an ending serves, gestures at or refuses, and find a carrier that serves several at once.
  3. Trace every load-bearing element of an ending to its planting, and distinguish a missing plant from missing pressure.
  4. Decide how long an aftermath should be, and where it should stop.
  5. Distinguish a declined question from a dropped one, and a chosen ambiguity from an evasion.

Key terms

Ending; climax and aftermath; desis and lusis; resolution and closure; termination; the retroactive lens; fictions of concord; closural allusion, terminal modification and unqualified assertion; anticipation of retrospection; privileged position; the narratable; the five domains (plot resolution, character-arc completion, thematic closure, emotional catharsis, the final image) and the proposed formal closure; earned and unearned; deposit and pressure; inevitability and predictability; the plant audit; the over-determined ending; the proportion problem and the last-decision rule; closural strategy; series hook. Each is defined at first use and collected in the consolidated glossary.

Contents

The first files say what an ending is and why it weighs more than its length, the middle files what it owes and how it pays, and the last are for use at the desk.

  1. The Problem
    A household ending that arrives in the post, and the chapter’s claim
  2. Definitions
    The ending, climax and aftermath, resolution and closure, and the terms around them
  3. How It Works
    Why readers want endings they know are artificial, what makes a last line final, why the end is read backwards, and the case against closure
  4. The Taxonomy
    Five domains an ending can serve, a proposed sixth, and rulings on proposals from earlier chapters
  5. The Mechanism
    Earned and unearned, inevitability, multiplicity at the close, and the proportion of climax to aftermath
  6. Diagnostic Tests
    Ten questions on one page, and how to read their failures together
  7. Failure Patterns
    Five recurrent failures, each with a work that commits it and succeeds
  8. Worked Analyses
    Great Expectations, Beloved, Never Let Me Go, Six Feet Under and the household revision case
  9. Checklist and Exercises
    The revision checklist and five exercises for your own draft
  10. Coda
    What the model assumes, and what an ending gives in exchange

A writer in revision can start at the diagnostic tests and the failure patterns; the case against closure in How It Works is best read before either, because the tests are calibrated to endings that conclude.

The full treatment

The Reference layer holds the complete chapter, The Ending: Kermode, Herrnstein Smith, Brooks, Torgovnick, DuPlessis and Miller in full, the cognitive evidence and its limits, sixteen closural strategies, twelve failure patterns, nine close readings (among them Middlemarch, The Great Gatsby, Anna Karenina, Nineteen Eighty-Four, Ulysses, Don Quixote and Blindness), the ending in the short story, the novella, the series and on screen, the household plant audit across the whole book, seminar questions and the sources.

See also: The Purposes of a Scene (Chapter 1), whose value shift the ending makes at the largest scale; Scene and Sequel (Chapter 2), whose sequel is the shape of an aftermath; The Opening Pages (Chapter 9), the formal inverse of this chapter; The Subplot (Chapter 14), whose attention tax the ending refunds; The Structural Edit (Chapter 18), which puts the ending’s audit into a revision order for the whole book.
