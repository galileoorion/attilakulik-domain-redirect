Chapter 1 · The Purposes of a Scene

The Purposes of a Scene

Continue in Reference

What a scene is for, and how to tell whether it is doing it

The Craft Behind the Story (Second Edition) — Chapter 1. Part I: The Scene and Its Materials.

In brief. A scene earns its place in a novel by changing something. Ask of each scene in a draft what was true when it opened and is no longer true when it closes; if the honest answer is “nothing”, the passage is setting rather than scene. The change can happen in five domains — the action, a person, the book’s meaning, the reader’s experience of the world, or what lies between two people — and a well-built scene usually moves two or three of them with the same material. The chapter’s tools are a two-sentence statement of the change, an audit of those five registers, a deliberate choice of narrative speed, and ten diagnostic questions. Use them one scene at a time when revising a finished draft, not while drafting. A scene that fails them may still belong in the book; its author should be able to say why.

What you can do after this chapter

  1. State, for any scene in your draft, what changed in it, in two parallel sentences.
  2. Say which of the five registers a scene serves and which it merely occupies, and find the second job a single-purpose scene could do without growing longer.
  3. Decide whether a piece of material should be a scene at all, or a paragraph of summary, an ellipsis or a habitual passage.
  4. Diagnose a flat scene by its mechanism (no want, no resistance, no change, redundancy, wrong speed) rather than by how it feels.
  5. Recognise when a scene that fails every test is the right choice for the book.

Key terms

Scene, beat, sequence; summary, duration; kernel and satellite; focalisation; value shift; period convention; the five domains of purpose and the relational domain; multiplicity; the scene–summary continuum, iterative narration, substitution testing; the reverse outline. Each is defined at first use and collected in the consolidated glossary.
