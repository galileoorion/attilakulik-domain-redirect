Exercises

These five exercises are for your own draft. Each produces a page of writing or a table, and none of them needs a finished book.

Exercise 1: two sentences per scene. Take one chapter of your draft. For every scene in it, write the start-and-close sentences of the value shift, in the same grammatical form. Where you cannot write them, write instead what the scene is for, and decide whether that is a reason the book needs.

Exercise 2: the domain audit. For the same chapter, build the table described under “The domain audit” in The Mechanism: length, domain letters, and three or four words of annotation for each letter. Strike every letter you cannot annotate. Choose the longest single-letter scene and find its latent second domain without adding more than fifty words.

Exercise 3: three speeds. Choose a scene that is not working. Rewrite it as singulative summary of no more than three sentences, and again as an ellipsis (the scene before it ending, the scene after it beginning, with nothing between). Read all three versions in place, in the chapter, before deciding which stays.

Exercise 4: the object between them. Choose a scene in which two characters’ relation is meant to change. Remove every sentence that states the relation. Give them one object to pass, withhold or refuse, and let the change happen through it.

Exercise 5: the reverse outline. Write one line per scene for your whole draft, in the language of consequence, with lengths and domain letters. Read it straight through and mark redundant pairs, runs of six lines in which a domain goes dark, and the point at which the book’s first movement ends.

See also: the Reference checklist and studio work, with further exercises and seminar questions.
